The underlying assets¶

A bank has placed 10 speculative grade corporate bonds in a CDO it is looking to sell. Each bond is 5 years in duration, makes quarterly coupon payments at an annual coupon rate of 6%, and the annual default probability is 4%. The LGD is 60% for all bonds. The market YTM on the bonds is 9%, and the risk-free interest rate is 4% per year. The “correlation” between any two bonds’ default dates is equal to a constant of 0.20. The bonds’ face values are $10 MM each.

The CDO¶

The structuring group has proposed two PACs for the CDO. The Class A tranche has an initial notional value of $15 MM and a coupon rate of 4.5% per year. The Class B tranche has a notional value of $30 MM and a coupon rate of 5.5% per year. The bank will retain the residuals as equity.

The Waterfall¶

The waterfall is as follows: Class A is paid first from available cash flows, and then Class B is paid. The residual goes to the bank. There are no carry-forwards, all cash flows are distributed as earned. Suggestion: Compute promised cash flows of the collateral, adjust the cash flows for default times, and distribute the cash flows according to the waterfall.

Task 1.¶

Compute the scheduled cash flows (waterfall) for all the tranches. Initially you may assume that Class A has $15 MM in principal (target rate 4.5%) and Class B has $30 MM at target rate 5.5%. The risk-free interest rate is 4% p.a.

Task 2.¶

Prepare 1000 sims from your static random numbers. This number is normally considered low, but since you have matched the moments, you will get better performance with a small number of sims.

Task 3.¶

Compute the PV of each tranche in each scenario at the risk-free rate. Compute the covariance matrix of the three assets' cash flows. Also compute a default indicator for future optimization of the structure.

Task 4.¶

Find the implicit cost of the risk using the bond valuations, and the cost per unit risk.

Task 5.¶

Value the tranches using the Lintner method.

Task 6.¶

BONUS: Optimize the structure to give the highest value to the residual, holding the Tranche A principal at $15 MM.

Assumptions¶

Each bond’s default time is modeled as an exponentially distributed random variable. If a bond defaults during a given quarter, the associated recovery payment is received in the immediately following quarter.

  • Step 2: Moment matching

A sample of 1,000 standard normal draws will not have a mean of exactly 0 and a standard deviation of exactly 1. Small deviations shift each bond's simulated default rate away from its true value. For example, a column mean of −0.06 raises the 5-year default probability from about 18.5% to about 20.2%. That would overstate expected losses purely because of sampling noise.

To remove this sampling error, each column (bond) is rescaled to have exactly zero mean and unit standard deviation:

$$\tilde{z}_{i,j} = \frac{z_{i,j} - \bar{z}_j}{s_j}$$

where $\bar{z}_j$ and $s_j$ are the sample mean and standard deviation of bond $j$'s 1,000 draws.

This adds no new information. It removes a sampling accident whose correct answer we already know. It also makes Part 2's parameter comparisons cleaner: the moment-matched draws are reused across all scenarios, so differences in results reflect parameter changes rather than noise.

  • Step 4: Correlated normals via Cholesky decomposition

To give every pair of bonds a correlation of $\rho = 0.20$, we follow the Cholesky method. The target correlation matrix $R$ is $10 \times 10$ with 1 on the diagonal and 0.20 elsewhere. Since each variable has unit variance, $R$ is also the covariance matrix $\Sigma$. Factor it as

$$R = LL^T$$

where $L$ is lower triangular. Then

$$Y = L \cdot Z, \quad Z = (Z_1, \dots, Z_{10}) \text{ iid } N(0,1)$$

produces correlated standard normals (the mean vector is $\mu = 0$). This works because $\text{Cov}(LZ) = L \cdot I \cdot L^T = LL^T = R$. The factorization exists because $R$ is positive definite.

We verify that $LL^T$ recovers $R$, and that the empirical correlation of the 1,000 simulated cases matches the 0.20 target within sampling noise.

  • Step 5: Correlated default times (Gaussian copula, exponential marginals)

The correlated normals are converted to default times in two steps:

  1. Map to correlated uniforms with the standard normal CDF: $U_{i,j} = \Phi(Y_{i,j})$.
  2. Apply the exponential inverse CDF: $\tau_{i,j} = -\ln(1 - U_{i,j})/\lambda$.

The hazard rate is set so that the annual default probability is exactly 4%: $\lambda = -\ln(1 - 0.04) \approx 0.0408$. We use $1 - U$ (rather than $U$) so that a low normal draw maps to an early default, consistent with the worst-to-best ordering in Step 3.

Default times are converted to quarters ($\tau \times 4$). A bond defaults in quarter $\lceil 4\tau \rceil$ if $4\tau \le 20$, and survives to maturity otherwise. Each bond's marginal default probability is unchanged by the correlation; what changes is that defaults cluster in the same cases, which fattens the tail of the number-of-defaults distribution.

  • Step 6: Bond cash flows

Each bond's promised schedule is a quarterly coupon of $10\text{MM} \times 6\%/4 = 0.15\text{MM}$, plus the 10MM face value at Q20. Following the defaultable bond model in §5.4, a default in quarter $d$ truncates this schedule:

Situation Cash flow ($MM)
Quarters before $d$ 0.15 coupon
Default quarter $d$ Recovery $(1 - 60\%) \times 10 = 4$, no coupon
After $d$ 0
No default 0.15 each quarter, plus 10 at Q20

The result is a 1,000 × 10 × 20 array (cases × bonds × quarters). show_case(n) displays the 10 × 20 cash-flow table for any case $n$ from 1 to 1,000.

  • Step 7: Portfolio cash flows

Summing the 10 bonds' cash flows gives the portfolio's quarterly cash flows: a 1,000 × 20 table (cases × quarters).

Check against the analytic expectation With survival probability $S(t) = e^{-\lambda t}$, the expected cash flow of one bond in quarter $q$ is

$$E[CF_q] = 0.15 \cdot S\!\left(\tfrac{q}{4}\right) + 4 \cdot \left[S\!\left(\tfrac{q-1}{4}\right) - S\!\left(\tfrac{q}{4}\right)\right] \;\big(+\,10 \cdot S(5) \text{ at } q = 20\big)$$

that is, the coupon if the bond survives the quarter, plus recovery if it defaults during the quarter. The portfolio expectation is 10 times this. The simulated mean across the 1,000 cases should match it closely, and both lie below the promised schedule (1.5MM per quarter, 101.5MM at Q20) because of expected default losses.

Step 2: Implicit cost of the risk (risk charge) and implicit cost per unit of risk

To value the CDO tranches using the Lintner model, we first need to determine the aggregate risk charge of the underlying portfolio. The risk charge represents the total market compensation required for bearing the collateral's default risk.

The formula is defined as:

$$\text{Risk Charge} = \sum E[PV(\text{Tranche}_{i})] - PV(\text{Collateral})$$

Where:

$\sum E[PV(\text{Tranche}_{i})]$: The sum of the expected present values of all CDO tranches (Class A, Class B, and Equity). The simulated cash flows for these tranches are discounted at the risk-free rate ($r_{f} = 4\%$).

$PV(\text{Collateral})$: The expected present value of the underlying default-free bond portfolio, discounted at the market Yield to Maturity ($YTM = 9\%$).

Task 5¶

  • Value the tranches using the Lintner method.
  1. Risk Adjustment per Tranche

We calculate the specific risk penalty for each individual tranche. A tranche's risk adjustment is proportional to its covariance with the total CDO portfolio.

$$\text{Risk Adjustment} = \lambda \cdot (\Sigma \cdot \mathbf{1})$$

Where:

$\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix of the tranches.

$\mathbf{1}$ is a column vector of ones.

The matrix multiplication $(\Sigma \cdot \mathbf{1})$ calculates the row sums of the covariance matrix, which represents each tranche's covariance with the aggregate portfolio's cash flows.

  1. Lintner Value

Finally, the Lintner risk-adjusted value for each tranche is its expected present value (discounted at the risk-free rate) minus its specific risk adjustment.

$$V_{\text{Lintner}} = E[PV_{\text{Tranche}}] - \text{Risk Adjustment}$$

Task 6¶

  • Optimize the structure to give the highest value to the residual, holding the Tranche A principal at $15 MM.

While a purely unconstrained mathematical optimization might suggest eliminating Class B entirely to create a massive Equity bucket (maximizing absolute dollar value), this structure is economically unviable.

Therefore, the true optimal structure maximizes the Equity value subject to realistic risk constraints. We achieve this by using the simulated default indicator to size the Equity tranche precisely to the collateral's tail risk.

Optimization Methodology

  1. Determine the Target Risk Percentile Using the 1,000 simulated scenarios, we evaluate the distribution of total defaults. We select a high confidence interval (e.g., the 95th percentile) to ensure the senior tranches are adequately protected from tail events.

$$\text{Target Defaults} = \text{Percentile}_{95}(\text{Simulated Defaults})$$

  1. Calculate Expected Tail Loss Next, we translate these targeted defaults into a dollar loss. Since each underlying bond has a $10 MM face value and a 60% Loss Given Default (LGD), each default results in exactly a $6.0 MM principal shortfall.

$$\text{Expected Tail Loss} = \text{Target Defaults} \times (\$10\text{ MM} \times \text{LGD})$$

  1. Analytically Size the Tranches The Equity tranche acts as the CDO's shock absorber. To protect Class B from our targeted tail risk, the Equity principal must be sized to exactly match the Expected Tail Loss. The remaining collateral is then allocated to Class B.

$$\text{Equity Principal} = \text{Expected Tail Loss}$$

$$\text{Class B Principal} = \text{Total Collateral} - \text{Class A} - \text{Equity Principal}$$
